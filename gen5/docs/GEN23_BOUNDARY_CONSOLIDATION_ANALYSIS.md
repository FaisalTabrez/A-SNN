# Gen-23 boundary consolidation analysis

Date analyzed: 2026-08-20

The supplied artifact manifest was verified before analysis. Gen-23 stopped
with zero qualifying seeds and closes the readout-level dual-memory program.

Selective boundary consolidation retained lesion A better than ordinary
single-memory adaptation (35.7175% versus 33.7700%, +1.9475 points), and LTW
removal caused a real 6.57-point loss. That stability came at a 4.93-point
lesion-B cost. More importantly, utility selection was worse than shuffled
selection by 1.35375 points on the registered joint score. The bounded-STW arm
was essentially identical to the single-memory control.

This is evidence that freezing readout weights can preserve an old condition;
it is not evidence that the proposed magnitude-selected consolidation rule
finds useful pathways or improves the stability-plasticity tradeoff. Together
with Gen-21 and Gen-22, the result rules out promotion of readout-level dual
memory into the backbone under the current protocol.

The valid project claims remain narrower: residual LIF state has a replicated
causal contribution on SHD/SSC, while topology, learned delays, local reward
credit, and both tested dual-memory rules have not passed their controlled
causal gates.
